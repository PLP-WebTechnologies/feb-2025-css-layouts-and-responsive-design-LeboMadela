Clinical Trial Phases

Clinical trials are conducted in phases, each with a specific purpose and set of objectives. The phases are:

  • Phase 1: Tests the safety and dosage of a drug.
  • Phase 2: Evaluates the effectiveness and side effects.
  • Phase 3: Confirms effectiveness, monitors side effects, and compares to commonly used treatments.
  • Phase 4: Post-marketing studies to gather additional information.

Phase 1

Phase 1 trials are the first step in testing a new drug or treatment in humans. The main goal is to evaluate the safety and determine the appropriate dosage.
These trials typically involve a small number of healthy volunteers and are closely monitored for any adverse effects.
Phase 1 trials are crucial for identifying the maximum tolerated dose and understanding how the drug is metabolized in the body.
They help researchers gather important data that will inform the design of subsequent phases of the trial.

Phase 2

Phase 2 trials are conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of a drug or treatment in patients with the target condition.
These trials typically involve a larger group of participants and are designed to assess the drug's efficacy and side effects.
Phase 2 trials help researchers determine the optimal dose and treatment regimen for further testing in Phase 3.
They also provide valuable information about the drug's safety profile and potential interactions with other medications.
